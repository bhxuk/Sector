Everything your hardware reports.

Sector reads the same sensors as the best monitoring tools, keeps the history, and tells you in plain words what it means.

Drive temperatures and health

Every hard drive and SSD: live temperature, SMART attributes, bad sectors, power-on time, data written, and a health verdict you can act on.

CPU, graphics and motherboard

Core and package temperatures, clocks, load and power, graphics card temperature and memory, and every motherboard sensor.

Fan speeds

Every fan the motherboard can see, in RPM, with how hard it's being driven. Get an alert if one stops.

A year of history

Recorded around the clock by a background service. Chart any period from an hour to a year and see the lowest, average and peak.

Alerts that make sense

Hot drives, CPUs and graphics cards, new bad sectors, stopped fans and PCs that drop offline. Your limits, per drive if you like.

A live number by the clock

Your hottest drive's temperature in the notification area, turning amber and red as it nears your limits.

Built for the NAS that runs hot.

Install Sector on your Windows NAS too, and watch every drive in it from your desktop, over your home network or Tailscale.

  1. Install Sector on the NASTick "Let my other PCs see this PC". It only answers your local network and Tailscale.
  2. Copy its pairing keyFrom Sector > Machines on the NAS.
  3. Add it on your desktopType its Tailscale name or IP and the key. It appears in the sidebar with its hottest drive beside it.
  4. Leave it runningThe NAS keeps its own history day and night, and your desktop warns you when a drive gets too warm.

How hot is too hot?

Sector starts with sensible limits. Change them, or set one per drive.

WarnCriticalWhy
Hard drives45°C50°CThey last longest below about 45°C. Months in the high 40s is a sign to add airflow.
SSDs and NVMe65°C75°CThey run warmer, and slow themselves down at around 70 to 80°C.
CPU85°C95°CModern CPUs boost right up to their limit, so short peaks are normal.
Graphics83°C90°CMost cards slow down at around 83 to 90°C.
